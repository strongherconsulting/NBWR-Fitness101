Start Here

This is a foundation round. You repeat it as many times as you like, and you raise your weights and intervals only when every session feels controlled and you recover well. Your body sets the pace. Our goal is strength, stamina, balance and bone health that last for decades.

What you need

  • Two pairs of dumbbells: light and moderate
  • One resistance band
  • A sturdy chair
  • A low step or the bottom stair
  • A wall and a counter for support
  • Comfortable shoes and a place to walk
  • A step counter or phone

Your daily rhythm

  • Morning: water, then your 9-minute Lymphatic Flow
  • Your strength, cardio or recovery session
  • A 30-minute daily walk, separate from meal walks
  • A 10–15 minute walk after each meal
  • Eat within your chosen window
  • Hydrate through the day
  • Post your progress to the group

How to read the numbers

  • Strength: 2–3 sets of 8–12 repetitions, resting 45–90 seconds
  • Effort scale (1–10): begin at 3–5, build toward 5–7
  • Finish each set with 2–4 good repetitions in reserve
  • Never hold your breath, jerk, or train to failure

The five daily pillars

  • Move: your session and your 9-minute morning flow
  • Walk: 30 minutes daily, plus after every meal, building toward 10,000 steps
  • Fast with intention: a daily eating window
  • Eat for nutrient density: protein, omega-3 fats, colorful plants
  • Hydrate: steady fluids and minerals all day

Your 9-Minute Tai Chi–Inspired Lymphatic Flow

Do this every morning before your session. It is nine slow, continuous movements, one minute each, flowing from one to the next the way a Tai Chi form does.

Why we move lymph

Your lymphatic system carries fluid, immune cells and cellular waste away from your tissues. Unlike your blood, it has no pump of its own. It relies on your breathing, your muscles contracting and your joints moving. When we sit for long periods, fluid can pool, and many of us feel it as heaviness, puffiness or stiffness.

A short morning flow wakes up that pumping action. It supports your body’s natural clearance of waste and excess fluid, helps circulation, and prepares your joints for the day. It is a gentle, supportive practice, and it works alongside hydration, good food, sleep and walking.

4Side-to-Side Torso SwingsTurn your torso left and right, letting your arms swing loosely like dead weight.Back: keep the twist very small, hips facing forward, moving only the upper torso.
5Golf SwingsSweep both arms up and overhead to one side, pivoting hips and feet, alternating sides.Foot or joint: no foot pivot. Keep your lower body still and use a smaller, slower upper-body sweep.
6Shoulder Rolls and ShrugsRoll shoulders back in large circles for 30 seconds, forward for 30 seconds. Then shrug to your ears and release.If shoulders ache, fingertips on shoulders and draw small circles with your elbows.
7Gentle Neck TiltsSlowly drop ear to shoulder, then chin to chest, alternating. Do not roll your head backward.Use slow, straight movements: look left, look right, tilt left, tilt right. No full circles.
8Hip CirclesHands on hips. Rotate your pelvis in large, smooth circles: 30 seconds each direction.Feet wider than hip-width for a stable base. Keep the circles small and controlled.

Optional Extensions

Add these one-minute movements when you want a longer flow. They target the areas where lymph nodes gather.

MovementHow to do it
Collarbone TappingCross your arms and gently tap the hollows just above your collarbones with two fingers. This is where lymph returns to the bloodstream.
Armpit SweepsRaise one arm. With the opposite flat hand, sweep gently from the elbow through the armpit and down the side of the ribs. Repeat on both sides.
Seated Hip-Crease PumpingSit and place the heels of your hands where thighs meet hips. Apply gentle, rhythmic pressure and release to encourage lower-body circulation.

Placement in your day

  • Do the flow first, then begin your session. Your session warm-up can then be 3–5 minutes.
  • On recovery days, the flow plus gentle mobility can be your whole session.
  • Move slowly and breathe through your nose when you can.
Please check with your clinician first if you have lymphedema, have had lymph nodes removed, are receiving cancer treatment, have a current infection, or have unexplained swelling. Skip any movement that causes pain.

Daily Walking: 30 Minutes Plus After Every Meal

Walking is the most important habit in this program. You will do two kinds, and they work together.

Your 30-minute daily walk

  • One dedicated 30-minute walk, separate from your meal walks
  • Choose your best time: early morning light or late afternoon work well
  • Easy-to-brisk pace where you can still hold a conversation
  • Outdoors when possible. Morning daylight supports sleep and energy

Your meal walks

  • Walk 10–15 minutes after every meal, starting within 10–30 minutes of finishing
  • Muscles use glucose during walking, which helps steady blood sugar
  • Keep it easy. A relaxed walk beats a rushed one
  • Keep these on rest days too

Building toward 10,000 steps

Your 30-minute walk adds roughly 3,000–4,000 steps, and each meal walk adds about 1,000–1,500. Together with ordinary daily movement, that brings you to 10,000.

  • Week 1: 6,000–7,000 steps, or your comfortable best
  • Week 2: 7,500 steps
  • Week 3: 9,000 steps
  • Week 4: 10,000 steps
  • If you are recovering from an injury, follow minutes rather than steps and build gradually

The 30-Day Program

How each session is built

  • Morning flow (9 minutes): your Tai Chi–inspired lymphatic routine
  • Warm-up and mobility (3–5 minutes): joint circles, hip openers, posture
  • Strength floor (15–25 minutes): legs, hips, back, chest, shoulders, arms, core
  • Cardio intervals (8–15 minutes): brisk walking, marching or easy cycling against an easier recovery pace
  • Balance and recovery (5–7 minutes): single-leg stance, stretching, slow breathing

Begin with shorter sessions of 25–30 minutes and build toward 45–55 minutes. Not every session needs every block at full length.

Week 1: Foundation and Movement Quality

Days 1–7. Learn the movements, build confidence, and set your baseline.

Step focus: 6,000–7,000 steps, or your comfortable best

Strength

Begin with your 9-minute Lymphatic Flow, then:

Moving up each round

  • Build repetitions first: from 8, to 10, to 12
  • When every repetition is controlled and you recover well, move to the next-smallest weight. Change one variable at a time
  • Lengthen your cardio work periods before shortening recovery periods
  • Use your Day 29 retest as your scorecard each round
  • Round 2: add one set to Week 1–2 strength exercises and lengthen cardio work intervals by 15–30 seconds
  • Round 3: move to the next weight, add a 3-second lowering tempo, and introduce single-leg variations

Fasting, Food and Hydration

Your eating window

  • Weeks 1–2: 16/8, eating within an 8-hour window such as 11:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m.
  • Weeks 3–4 and later rounds: 18/6 as energy stays steady
  • Advanced rounds: 20/4 or one meal a day (OMAD)
  • Prioritize protein at every meal as your window shortens
  • Break your fast with protein and vegetables before starches
  • Progress only when you feel steady, sleep well and train with good energy

Nutrient-dense eating

  • Protein: a palm to two palms per meal, from wild fish, pasture-raised eggs, poultry, grass-fed meat, Greek yogurt or legumes
  • Omega-3 fats: wild salmon, sardines, mackerel, anchovies, walnuts, chia, flax and hemp, with fatty fish 2–3 times weekly
  • Improve your omega-6 to omega-3 ratio: reduce refined seed oils and ultra-processed foods, and cook with olive or avocado oil
  • Half your plate: colorful vegetables and berries
  • Bone support: leafy greens, sardines with bones, yogurt, nuts
  • Gut support: kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi

Peak hydration

  • Begin the day with 16–20 ounces of water
  • Spread fluids through the day, with extra around training and walks
  • Add a pinch of quality sea salt and lemon, especially in longer fasts
  • Herbal tea, mineral water, and unsweetened coffee or green tea in moderation
  • Pale-yellow urine is a simple daily check
  • Ease fluids in the last hour before bed
Please consult your physician before changing your eating window if you take medication for diabetes or blood pressure, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating. Extended fasting and OMAD are not suited to everyone.

Bone, Balance and Longevity

  • Progressive resistance, weight-bearing movement and balance practice all support bone health. Weights alone are not enough, which is why daily walking matters.
  • Cycling is excellent for the heart but loads the bones less than walking.
  • This program avoids high-impact jumps and fast, uncontrolled movement.
  • If you have osteoporosis, a prior fragility fracture, significant back pain or a balance impairment, ask your clinician or physical therapist to tailor the program before adding spinal flexion, twisting or impact work.
  • This program is educational and does not replace medical advice. Please consult your physician before beginning any exercise or nutrition program.
